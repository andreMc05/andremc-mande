You are already dead.

In *Fist of the North Star*, Kenshiro’s Hokuto Shinken does not kill on contact. It strikes the body’s pressure points. The opponent keeps moving, talking, believing the fight is still open. Only later does the delayed damage finish them. The line is simple: “Omae wa mou shindeiru.”

Most software, software services, and anything built on software is in that window. The pressure points have already been hit. The body is still standing. The explosion has not finished.

**Creative software suites**  
In early October 2026, one developer used Claude Opus 5.5 to produce seven open-source Rust applications positioned as clean-room reimplementations of Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, Lightroom, After Effects, InDesign, and Acrobat. PhotoCraft alone crossed roughly 43,000 GitHub stars within days. The apps remain early alpha. They already demonstrate that a decades-old, high-margin creative suite can be recreated at usable interface and file-format fidelity by a single experienced engineer plus a frontier coding model in a matter of days, not years. Adobe had recently settled a large federal case over its subscription practices; the project’s author cited those practices as motivation.  

Pressure-point reading: The scarcity of skilled labor and the multi-year cost of building a competitive creative suite have been struck. The subscription model can continue collecting revenue. It no longer rests on the assumption that a capable alternative requires a large team and a long timeline.

**Video production and franchise expansion**  
Leading models in October 2026 (Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, Kling, MiniMax Hailuo, and others) generate short clips that reviewers and preference arenas describe as cinema-quality, often with native audio and usable character or scene consistency. Feature-length or near-feature AI-assisted films have already premiered or been released. Fan filmmakers are producing trailers, expansions, and short works set inside existing universes at costs that have collapsed for many shot types that previously required expensive VFX. Continuity across long form remains a human craft problem, and exact recreation of protected IP still triggers takedowns.  

Pressure-point reading: The capital and specialized labor barriers that once protected the visual language of major franchises have been struck for short-to-medium form and for fan expansion. Official productions still control the highest-end continuity and legal clearance. The assumption that high-quality franchise-adjacent footage requires a traditional VFX pipeline no longer holds for large classes of work.

**Recorded music**  
Major labels sued Suno and Udio in 2024 over training on copyrighted recordings. Some have since settled and entered licensing partnerships; others continue litigation, including a second suit filed against Suno in September 2026 covering later models and large numbers of additional tracks. Independent artists have filed class actions. The American Federation of Musicians has sued labels over the distribution of AI-licensing revenue. Sony has issued hundreds of thousands of AI deepfake takedown requests. Fair-use claims remain contested.  

Pressure-point reading: The economic model that treated recorded performances as scarce, tightly controlled assets has been struck. Labels can still litigate, settle, and extract new revenue. The prior assumption that generative tools could not produce competitive output without permission, or that the industry could restore the old control regime through enforcement alone, no longer holds.

**Security and sealed systems**  
Google’s Threat Intelligence Group reported in September 2026 that monthly vulnerability disclosures roughly doubled during the year. Vulnerabilities identified as likely AI-discovered showed a higher rate of remote-code-execution outcomes (50 % versus 26 % for non-AI findings in the analyzed set). At least one autonomously discovered flaw (CVE-2026-1731) was exploited within days of disclosure. Benchmarks such as ExploitGym show frontier agents successfully turning real-world vulnerabilities into working exploits even when common defenses are present. Multi-turn and agentic jailbreaks continue to succeed against frontier models themselves.  

Pressure-point reading: Environments previously treated as adequately sealed—proprietary software, enterprise tools, model guardrails—have been struck. AI is both finding more (and more severe) flaws in conventional systems and being used to bypass the controls on AI systems. The delay between discovery and weaponization has shortened in documented cases.

**Technical debt and hidden bugs**  
The same capabilities apply to existing codebases. Coding agents have refactored a 300,000-line C codebase (an open-source *Street Fighter III* decompilation) over three weeks, raising its Code Health score from 5.6 to 10.0 while preserving behavior through replay traces. Large language ports—hundreds of thousands to a million lines—have been completed in days to weeks with full test suites passing before merge. Agents can both patch known vulnerabilities and proactively rewrite code to eliminate classes of risk. At the same time, adversaries are using models to accelerate analysis of patches and public disclosures into working exploits.  

The practical choice is no longer “maintain forever versus a multi-year rewrite.” Full clean-room or language-migration rebuilds are now feasible on timelines measured in weeks when verification harnesses exist. Hidden business rules still require characterization tests; unconstrained generation can also introduce new forms of debt. For many systems the rational path remains incremental modernization. For others, the cost of commissioning a lower-debt implementation of known behavior has collapsed.  

Pressure-point reading: Software carrying technical debt and latent flaws has already been struck. AI can accelerate the delayed damage by finding and exploiting those points, or it can perform the repair at a speed that was previously unavailable. Continuing to build on the old body means investing in a system whose pressure points are now machine-readable. Rebuilding means the multi-year scarcity assumption that once made rewrites impractical has itself been struck.

The explosion is not a single simultaneous collapse. It is the accelerating erosion of the old scarcity assumptions: skilled labor, capital barriers, multi-year timelines, security through imperfect sealing, and control over content and code. Incumbents can still operate, collect revenue, and litigate. The foundational assumptions that made those positions durable have already been hit.

Omae wa mou shindeiru.
